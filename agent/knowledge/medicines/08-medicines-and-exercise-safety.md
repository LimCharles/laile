# Everyday medicines and exercising safely

Many people take medicines for long-term conditions. Most are completely compatible with exercise, but a few change how your body responds. This guide explains what to be aware of.

**Never change or stop a medicine because of exercise. Talk to your doctor or pharmacist.**

## Blood pressure medicines

- **Examples:** amlodipine, nifedipine; lisinopril, perindopril, enalapril; losartan, valsartan, telmisartan; hydrochlorothiazide, indapamide.
- **Be aware:** some people feel dizzy when they stand up quickly. Rise slowly from lying or sitting. Take care with sit-to-stands and when getting up after floor exercises.
- **Water tablets (diuretics):** drink enough when exercising in the heat, unless your doctor has limited your fluids.

## Beta blockers

- **Examples:** bisoprolol, atenolol, metoprolol, propranolol.
- **Be aware:** they keep your heart rate lower, so heart rate is not a good guide to effort.
- **Use the talk test instead:** moderate effort means you can talk but not sing.

## Diabetes medicines

- **Low blood sugar (hypoglycaemia):** Exercise lowers blood sugar. With insulin, or tablets like gliclazide or glipizide (sulfonylureas), it can drop too low during or after exercise. **Signs:** shakiness, sweating, a fast heartbeat, hunger, confusion. **Be prepared:** keep a quick source of sugar nearby, and follow your care team's plan for checking your blood sugar.
- **Metformin on its own** rarely causes low blood sugar.
- **SGLT2 inhibitors** (for example empagliflozin, dapagliflozin): stay well hydrated.

## Cholesterol medicines (statins)

- **Examples:** atorvastatin, simvastatin, rosuvastatin.
- **Be aware:** rarely, statins cause muscle pain or weakness. Normal soreness after exercise is in the muscles you worked, and fades within a few days. Tell your doctor about muscle pain that is unexplained, all over, or with dark urine.

## Blood thinners

- **Examples:** warfarin, rivaroxaban, apixaban, dabigatran, clopidogrel, aspirin.
- **Be aware:** You may bruise more easily, and falls matter more. Choose steady exercises, and clear the floor around you. Report any bleeding signs; see the red flags guide.

## Asthma and breathing medicines

- **Be aware:** keep your reliever inhaler (for example salbutamol) nearby when you exercise.
- **Stop and follow your asthma plan if:** you become wheezy, tight-chested or breathless beyond normal effort.

## Medicines that cause drowsiness or dizziness

- **Examples:** strong painkillers, some sleep and anxiety medicines, some nerve pain medicines, and some older antihistamines.
- **Be aware:** these can raise the risk of a fall. Exercise when you feel most alert. Use support for standing exercises.

## Supplements

Supplements such as glucosamine, fish oil, turmeric and ginkgo can interact with other medicines, especially blood thinners. Tell your doctor or pharmacist what you take.
