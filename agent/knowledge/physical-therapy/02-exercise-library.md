# Laile exercise library

These are the exercises Laile can track with the camera. Each entry says:
- what the exercise is for;
- how to set up and do it;
- what is normal to feel;
- common mistakes;
- how to make it easier or harder.

Your physio's program, Lele's plan, or the Laile program you chose decides which ones you do, and how many.

## Knee and hip exercises (after knee or hip replacement, knee pain or arthritis)

### Ankle pumps
- **For:** keeping blood moving in your legs, which helps prevent clots after surgery. It is often done many times a day in the first weeks.
- **Setup:** lie on your back with your legs straight. Any camera view works.
- **How:** point your toes away, then pull them back towards you, in a steady rhythm.
- **Normal to feel:** your calf working.
- **Tell Lele or your care team:** a calf that is swollen, hot, red or painful. This is a warning sign; see the red flags guide.

### Quad set (thigh squeeze)
- **For:** waking up the thigh muscle and getting the knee fully straight.
- **Setup:** lie on your back with your legs straight, side-on to the phone.
- **How:** tighten your thigh and press the back of your knee down into the bed. Hold, then relax.
- **Measured:** the knee straightening gap. The timer only runs while your knee is straight enough.
- **Normal to feel:** your thigh working hard, and a stretch behind the knee.
- **Common mistake:** holding your breath. Breathe normally.

### Heel slides
- **For:** getting your knee to bend enough for chairs, stairs and getting into a car.
- **Setup:** lie on your back, with your operated leg on the side facing the phone.
- **How:** slide your heel towards your bottom, as far as is comfortable, then slide it back.
- **Measured:** knee bend. A rep counts when you reach the target bend.
- **Normal to feel:** a pulling or tight stretch at the front of the knee that eases when you straighten again.
- **Easier:** a smaller bend. Lele may lower the target after a sore session.
- **Tip:** a plastic bag or towel under the heel helps it slide.

### Straight-leg raise
- **For:** thigh strength, so your knee feels steady when you walk.
- **Setup:** lie on your back. Bend the other knee and keep the operated leg straight, side-on to the phone.
- **How:** tighten your thigh, lift the straight leg to the height of the other knee, then lower slowly.
- **Common mistake:** letting the knee bend as you lift. Lele reminds you to keep it straight.
- **Easier:** fewer reps, or lift only a little way.

### Seated knee straightening (long-arc quad)
- **For:** the thigh strength you need to stand up.
- **Setup:** sit on a firm chair, side-on to the phone.
- **How:** straighten your knee until your leg is level, pause, then lower slowly.
- **Easier:** straighten part-way.

### Sit-to-stand
- **For:** everyday strength for standing up from a chair or toilet; it keeps you independent.
- **Setup:** sit near the front of a sturdy chair, side-on to the phone, with your feet flat. After hip replacement, use a chair high enough to keep within your hip precautions.
- **How:** lean forward, stand all the way up, then sit back down slowly.
- **Easier:** use your hands on the armrests, or a higher chair.
- **Harder:** arms crossed, or a slower lowering.

### Mini squat
- **For:** leg strength without asking too much of the knee.
- **Setup:** stand side-on to the phone, holding a counter or the back of a sturdy chair.
- **How:** bend your knees a little, as if starting to sit, then stand tall.
- **Common mistake:** knees drifting inward. Keep them in line with your toes.

### Glute bridge
- **For:** the buttock muscles, which take pressure off the knees and lower back.
- **Setup:** lie on your back with knees bent and feet flat, side-on to the phone.
- **How:** squeeze your buttocks and lift your hips until your body is straight from knees to shoulders, then lower.
- **Common mistake:** arching the lower back. Lift with the buttocks.

## Shoulder exercises (stiff or painful shoulder)

### Pendulum swings
- **For:** loosening a stiff shoulder without asking the muscles to work.
- **Setup:** lean on a table with your good hand and let the sore arm hang down.
- **How:** rock your body gently so the arm swings in small circles or side to side. The arm stays relaxed.
- **Normal to feel:** a gentle loosening. It should not hurt.

### Forward arm raise
- **For:** the reach you need for shelves, doing your hair and getting dressed.
- **Setup:** stand side-on to the phone with your arms by your sides.
- **How:** lift your arm forward and up as high as is comfortable, thumb up and elbow fairly straight, then lower slowly.
- **Measured:** arm lift, in degrees. 180° is straight up. A rep counts when you reach your target height.
- **Normal to feel:** a stretch or mild ache at the top that eases as you lower.
- **Easier:** a lower target, or bend the elbow a little.
- **Common mistake:** hitching the shoulder up towards the ear. Keep it down and relaxed.

### Side arm raise
- **For:** moving the shoulder in the direction that stiffens most.
- **Setup:** face the phone with your arms by your sides and your whole body in view.
- **How:** lift your arm out to the side, thumb up, as high as is comfortable, then lower slowly.
- **Easier:** stop at shoulder height.

### Wall slides
- **For:** reaching higher with less effort, because the wall takes some of the arm's weight.
- **Setup:** stand facing a wall, side-on to the phone, fingertips on the wall at chest height.
- **How:** walk or slide your fingers up the wall as far as is comfortable, then slide back down.
- **Easier:** stand further from the wall, or stop lower.

### Shoulder blade squeeze
- **For:** the muscles that steady the shoulder, and undoing a desk slump.
- **Setup:** sit tall in a chair with your arms relaxed.
- **How:** squeeze your shoulder blades back and down, hold, then relax.
- **Common mistake:** shrugging. Think "back pockets", not "ears".

## Move exercises (everyday strength and stretching)

### Bodyweight squat
- **For:** the biggest muscles you have; the fastest way to stronger legs.
- **Setup:** stand side-on to the phone, with your feet shoulder-width apart.
- **How:** sit your hips back and down until your thighs are about level, then drive up.
- **Easier:** squat to a chair (sit-to-stand).
- **Harder:** slower lowering, or more reps.

### Push-up family (incline, knee, full)
- **For:** chest, shoulders, arms and core.
- **Levels:** Incline push-up: hands on a counter or table. Knee push-up: on the floor, knees down. Full push-up: on your toes.
- **How:** lower your chest towards your hands, keeping your body in a straight line, then push away.
- **Next level:** when you can do about 15 good reps, move on to the next version. A re-check then sets your new targets.
- **Common mistake:** sagging hips. Lele reminds you to keep your hips in line.
- **Watch out:** wrist pain. Tell Lele; the incline version puts less load on the wrists.

### Plank
- **For:** core strength that protects your back.
- **Setup:** forearms on the floor, body straight from head to heels, side-on to the phone.
- **How:** brace your stomach and hold. The timer pauses if your hips sag or pike.
- **Easier:** plank from your knees, or shorter holds.

### Wall sit
- **For:** leg endurance with zero impact.
- **How:** with your back against a wall, slide down until your knees are about at a right angle, and hold.
- **Easier:** don't slide down as far, or hold for less time.

### Reverse lunge
- **For:** single-leg strength and balance.
- **How:** step one foot back and lower until your front knee is bent to about a right angle, then step back up. Do both sides.
- **Easier:** hold on to a chair for balance, or do a shorter step.

### Jumping jacks
- **For:** a quick burst of cardio.
- **Setup:** face the phone, with your whole body in view.
- **How:** jump your feet out and swing your arms overhead, then back in.
- **Easier:** step out one foot at a time, with no jump.
- **Note:** high impact. Left out of physio programs, and whenever your limits rule out impact (for example, sore knees).

### High knees
- **For:** fast cardio in a small space.
- **How:** march or jog on the spot, bringing your knees up high.
- **Easier:** march instead of jogging.

### Deep squat hold
- **For:** undoing hours of sitting; hip and ankle mobility.
- **How:** sink into a comfortable deep squat and breathe. Hold on to something if you need to.

### Hamstring stretch
- **For:** loosening the back of the thighs, which tighten with sitting.
- **How:** put one heel on a low step with the leg straight, and lean forward from your hips until you feel a gentle stretch. Do both sides.

### Hip flexor stretch
- **For:** opening the front of the hips after long hours in a chair.
- **How:** kneel on one knee with the other foot in front. Tuck your tailbone under and shift forward gently. Do both sides.

### Chest opener
- **For:** undoing hunched-over-a-phone posture.
- **How:** stand or sit tall with your hands clasped behind your back. Squeeze your shoulder blades together and lift your chest.

### Neck and shoulder rolls
- **For:** releasing desk tension in the neck and shoulders.
- **How:** sit tall, roll your shoulders slowly backwards, then gently tilt your ear towards each shoulder.

## How Laile makes an exercise easier or harder

- **Your targets:** the first session of every program is a check-in, and your reps, holds and range are set from it. Redo it every couple of weeks to reset them.
- **Easier:** after a sore or painful moment, Lele lowers the target range, or shortens holds, for that exercise. See "Lele's notes". Your physio can also set lower targets or leave exercises out altogether.
- **Harder:** in Laile's calisthenics programs, each re-check raises your targets as you improve, and the push-up family has versions to move through (incline, knee, full).
- **Physio programs:** moving to a harder stage always needs your physio's sign-off. Your physio can ask Lele to draft the change, then signs it.
