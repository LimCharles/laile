# Red flags: when to stop and get help

Lele cannot examine you. These signs need a person to check you, not an app.

## Call an ambulance now (Singapore: 995)

- Chest pain, pressure or tightness.
- Sudden shortness of breath or trouble breathing, especially after surgery. This can be a clot in the lungs.
- Coughing up blood.
- Fainting, or feeling like you are about to faint and can't stay upright.
- Signs of a stroke: a face drooping, an arm or leg suddenly weak, slurred speech.
- Very heavy bleeding that won't stop.
- After a hip replacement: sudden severe hip pain with the leg looking shorter or turned, and you can't move it.
- Loss of control of the bladder or bowels together with back pain, or numbness around the genitals or bottom.

## Contact your care team or see a doctor the same day

- A calf that is swollen, warm, red or painful, or one leg suddenly more swollen than the other. This can be a clot in the leg (deep vein thrombosis).
- A wound that is red, hot, increasingly painful, oozing or leaking, or that has opened.
- Fever or chills.
- New numbness, tingling or weakness in the leg.
- Not being able to put weight on the leg when you could before.
- Dizziness or light-headedness.
- Signs of bleeding while on a blood thinner: blood in urine, black or tarry stools, vomiting blood, or large unexplained bruises.
- Pain that is suddenly much worse and not settling with rest.

## What Laile does

- If you mention any of these during a session, Laile ends the session and tells you what to do. The report is saved for your clinician.
- Laile checks for these words with fixed rules, so a warning sign is never left to the AI's judgement.
- In rehab mode, same-day concerns go to your care team. In Move mode, Laile tells you to see a doctor.

## If you are unsure

It is always okay to call your clinic, your surgeon's team, or a doctor. When in doubt, get checked.

## Feeling low

Recovery can be hard. If you feel very low, or have thoughts of harming yourself, please talk to someone. In Singapore, Samaritans of Singapore (SOS) is available 24 hours on 1767. In an emergency, call 995.
