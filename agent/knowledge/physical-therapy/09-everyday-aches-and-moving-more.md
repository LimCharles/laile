# Everyday aches and moving more

Laile's quick sessions and Lele's plans are for people who sit a lot and have little time. The idea: short, frequent movement beats rare long workouts you never get round to.

## How much movement helps

- The World Health Organization suggests that adults aim for 150 to 300 minutes a week of moderate activity (brisk walking counts), plus muscle-strengthening exercise on 2 or more days a week.
- Any amount is better than none. Short bursts ("exercise snacks") of 2 to 10 minutes add up.
- Breaking up long spells of sitting helps too: stand up and move for a minute or two every 30 to 60 minutes.

## Desk body: common aches

- **Stiff neck and shoulders:** try neck and shoulder rolls, and the chest opener. Set your screen at eye level.
- **Tight hips and lower back:** try the hip flexor stretch, the deep squat hold, and glute bridges.
- **Tight hamstrings:** try the hamstring stretch.
- **Stretches:** Hold for about 30 seconds and breathe. A stretch should feel like a gentle pull. Stop if it turns sharp.

## Getting stronger: progressions in Laile

- **Push-ups:** incline (hands on a counter) → knee push-ups → full push-ups.
- **Legs:** sit-to-stand → bodyweight squat → slower or deeper squats, and lunges.
- **Core:** plank from the knees → plank → longer holds.
- **When to move up:** when you can do the target reps with good form. A re-check then sets your new targets.
- **If a move hurts:** tell Lele. It notes it and eases that exercise next time.

## Low back pain

- Back pain is very common and usually improves within a few weeks.
- Staying gently active usually helps more than bed rest.
- **See a doctor if:** the pain follows a fall or injury; it comes with fever, unexplained weight loss, or leg weakness; it hasn't improved after a few weeks.
- **Call 995 now:** loss of bladder or bowel control, or numbness around the genitals or bottom.

## Knee and hip osteoarthritis

- Exercise is one of the main treatments for osteoarthritis: stronger muscles support the joint.
- Some discomfort during exercise is acceptable if it settles within a day.
- Low-impact options (cycling, swimming, walking, strength work) are usually well tolerated.

## Sprains and strains

- In the first days, protect the area and let pain guide you.
- Then gradually return to gentle movement and loading. Complete rest for long periods tends to slow recovery.
- **See a professional if:** you can't put weight on it, it's very swollen or misshapen, or it isn't improving.

## When you need a physio instead

- **After surgery, or with a condition a clinician is treating:** ask your clinician before starting new exercise. Their physio can give you a Laile code for a program they supervise.
- **Before starting a new exercise routine:** check with a doctor if you have heart problems, chest pain with exertion, dizziness or fainting, or have been told not to exercise.
