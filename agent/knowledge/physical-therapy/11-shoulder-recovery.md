# Stiff or painful shoulder

This guide covers the common shoulder problems Laile's physio programs help with: a stiff ("frozen") shoulder, and everyday shoulder pain from strain or overuse. Your physio's advice for your own shoulder always comes first.

## Stiff shoulder (frozen shoulder)

- A frozen shoulder slowly becomes painful and stiff, then slowly loosens again. It often affects people aged 40 to 60, and is more common with diabetes.
- It usually goes through stages: a painful "freezing" stage, a stiff "frozen" stage, and a "thawing" stage when movement returns. The whole course can take a year or more.
- Gentle, regular movement within a comfortable range helps keep and regain motion. Forcing the shoulder into sharp pain usually doesn't help.
- Little and often works best: a few minutes, once or twice a day.

## Everyday shoulder pain

- Many shoulder aches come from strain, overuse (lifting, reaching, long hours at a desk) or tight muscles around the shoulder blade.
- Keeping the shoulder moving gently, and strengthening the muscles that steady the shoulder blade, is usually part of getting better.
- See a doctor or physio if you can't lift your arm at all, the pain came on after a fall or injury, or it is getting worse.

## How Laile measures your shoulder

- **Arm lift** is how high your arm goes when you raise it forward, in degrees. Your arm by your side is about 0°, level with your shoulder is 90°, and straight up is about 180°.
- The camera measures it during forward arm raises, from a side view.
- Useful everyday milestones: about 90° to reach a table or a light switch, about 120° to reach a shelf or wash your hair, and about 150° to reach a high cupboard.
- Your first session is a check-in: Lele sets your target to 90% of the height you reached comfortably, so every rep is achievable.

## What's normal to feel

- A stretch or mild ache at the top of the movement that eases as you lower your arm is expected.
- Stiffness first thing in the morning, which loosens as you move, is common.
- Soreness that settles by the next morning usually means the amount was about right.

## Tell Lele or your physio

- Sharp or catching pain during a movement. Lele will stop the set and note it.
- Pain that wakes you at night and is getting worse.
- New pins and needles, numbness or weakness in the arm or hand.
- Soreness that is worse the next morning, two days running.

## Get help urgently

- Chest pain, especially with breathlessness, sweating, or pain spreading to the arm or jaw. This can be a heart problem. Call 995 in Singapore.
- A hot, red, swollen shoulder with fever.
- A shoulder that looks out of place after a fall, or an arm you suddenly can't move.

## Daily life tips

- Use the arm for light everyday tasks within comfort. Avoiding it completely tends to make stiffness worse.
- Keep things you use often on lower shelves while reach is limited.
- When dressing, put the stiff arm into sleeves first, and take it out last.
- Sitting tall with your shoulder blades gently back takes pressure off the front of the shoulder.

## After shoulder physio

When your physio discharges you, Lele suggests Shoulder mobility: wall slides, forward and side arm raises, and shoulder blade squeezes, with your arm lift measured so you can watch your reach keep improving. Any limits your physio chose to keep, such as a maximum arm height, still apply.
