# Recovering from a knee replacement

This is general information about the usual recovery after a total knee replacement. Everyone recovers at their own pace, and your surgeon and physiotherapist set your own targets and timeline. Follow their advice over this guide.

## The two things that matter most early on

1. **Straightening the knee fully.** A knee that stays slightly bent makes walking tiring and uneven. Quad sets and resting with the leg straight help. Laile measures this as the "knee straightening gap"; you want it close to 0°. Avoid resting with a pillow under the knee unless your team says so.
2. **Bending the knee.** Everyday life needs a good bend: roughly 65–70° for walking; around 90° or more to get out of a chair comfortably; more for stairs and getting in and out of a car; Heel slides are the main bending exercise, and Laile measures your bend each time.

## A typical timeline

This is only a rough picture; your clinician sets your stages.

- **First 2 weeks:** Control swelling and pain. Get the knee straight. Build bend step by step. Walk short distances with your walking aid. Do ankle pumps often.
- **Weeks 2 to 6:** More bend; many programs aim to pass 90° in this period. Stronger thigh and buttock muscles. Walk further, and often move from a frame to a stick. Swelling usually goes down but can come back after a busy day.
- **Weeks 6 to 12:** Strength and balance. Stairs. Longer walks. Getting back to hobbies. Swelling and warmth can last for months; that is common.
- **3 months and beyond:** steady gains continue for up to a year.

## Stages in Laile

Your clinician's program has stages. Each stage has a rule for moving on, for example "knee bend of 90° or more in 3 sessions in a row, with exercise pain of 3/10 or less".

- Lele checks your camera measurements against that rule and tells you what's met and what isn't.
- Moving to the next stage always needs your clinician's sign-off.

## If progress stalls

It's normal for knee bend to go up in steps, with flat patches.

- If it stays flat for more than a week or two, or goes backwards, Laile flags it to your clinician.
- A stiff knee is easier to help early, so it's worth raising.
- Common reasons include pain, swelling, worry about pushing the knee, or missed sessions. Lele will ask what's getting in the way.

## Swelling and warmth

- Some swelling and warmth are expected for weeks to months.
- They often increase after more activity.
- Your team may suggest raising the leg (elevation), a wrapped ice pack, or compression stockings.
- **Get checked:** swelling in the calf, or a wound that becomes red, hot or leaky. See the red flags guide.

## Kneeling, driving and returning to activities

- Your surgeon will tell you when it's safe to drive, to kneel, and to return to sport or work. The timing varies.
- Walking, swimming (once the wound has healed and your team agrees) and cycling are commonly encouraged later on.
- High-impact activities like running and jumping are often discouraged on a new knee. Ask your surgeon.

## Long-term: Move mode

When your clinician discharges you, Laile can switch you to Move mode.

- Quick strength and stretching sessions keep the new joint working well for years.
- Your clinician's remaining precautions still apply.
