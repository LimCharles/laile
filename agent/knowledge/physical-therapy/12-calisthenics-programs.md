# Calisthenics and Laile's programs

Calisthenics is strength training with your own bodyweight: squats, push-ups, lunges, bridges and planks. It needs no equipment, and the camera can count and check every rep.

## Laile's programs

- **Ten-minute strength:** squats, push-ups, glute bridges and planks. The full-body basics, about three times a week. Level 2.
- **First push-up:** incline push-ups on a counter, knee push-ups, planks and a chest opener. For anyone working towards their first full push-up. Level 1.
- **Squat strong:** squats, reverse lunges, wall sits and glute bridges, with squat depth checked by the camera. Legs that carry you up any stairs. Level 2.
- **Core foundations:** glute bridges, planks, and hip flexor and hamstring stretches. For anyone who sits a lot and wants a back that holds up to long days. Level 1.
- **Shoulder mobility:** wall slides, forward and side arm raises, shoulder blade squeezes and a chest opener, with arm lift measured. Good for desk hunches, and after shoulder physio. Level 1.
- **Strong knees for life:** chair stands, bridges, squats, lunges and a hamstring stretch. What comes after knee physio; any limits your physio set still apply. Level 1.

Quick sessions: Desk reset (2 minutes), Morning mobility (5 minutes) and Lunch-break blast (7 minutes, higher intensity).

## Lele's plans

- If none of these fit, Lele builds a plan from your goals, your time, your experience and anything that hurts.
- Sore knees leave out deep squats, jumping and high knees. Sore wrists leave out push-ups and planks. Sore shoulders limit how high your arms go. Recovering from an injury keeps everything low impact.
- If you mention pain or an injury, Lele keeps it gentle and suggests seeing a physiotherapist.

## How progress works

- **Check-in first:** the first session of every program sets your personal targets from what you can comfortably do, so a beginner and a regular exerciser start in the right place.
- **Working sets:** targets are about 70% of your best, so you finish each set with a little left in the tank. That's what builds strength without wrecking you for tomorrow.
- **Re-check:** every couple of weeks, redo the check-in from the Progress tab. Your targets go up as you do.
- **Levels:** in the push-up family, move from incline to knee to full push-ups once the easier version gets easy (about 15 good reps). First push-up has incline and knee push-ups in it; Ten-minute strength uses full push-ups.
- **Rest days:** strength programs work best about three times a week, with a day between. Quick sessions and stretching are fine every day.

## The push-up path

- **Incline push-up:** hands on a kitchen counter or sturdy table. The higher the surface, the easier it is.
- **Knee push-up:** on the floor with your knees down, body straight from knees to shoulders.
- **Full push-up:** on your toes, body straight from heels to shoulders.
- A common milestone: 10 good knee push-ups usually means a first full push-up is close.
- Wrist pain: tell Lele. Incline push-ups, or push-ups on your fists or on push-up handles, put less bend in the wrist.

## Good form, in plain words

- **Squat:** feet shoulder-width, sit back as if to a chair, knees in line with toes, chest up.
- **Lunge:** step back far enough that both knees bend to about a right angle; front knee over the ankle.
- **Plank and push-up:** a straight line, with no sagging or piking hips. Breathe.
- **Bridge:** squeeze your buttocks to lift and keep your lower back neutral.

## Normal and not normal

- Muscles feeling tired or warm, and mild soreness a day or two later (especially when starting), are normal.
- Sharp pain in a joint, pain that makes you change how you move, or pain that's worse the next morning is not. Tell Lele; it will ease that exercise and note it.
